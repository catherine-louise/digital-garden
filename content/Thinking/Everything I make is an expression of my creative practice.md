## Trying and failing to be creative in public

This digital garden is not the first time I've experimented with *being creative in semi-public*, but it feels more aligned than anything else I've tried. 

2024: Made a very complicated website on Webflow to hold poetry, flash fiction, creative reflections, zines, which ran aground for a few reasons:
- Webflow was far too complicated to maintain
- The amount of work required to post in the CMS (that I myself made, ha ha) made me feel things had to be Finished and Completed to post there, but of course I never felt like I wrote or worked fast enough, so I felt little momentum
- I was too schematic about what it should contain - too curated from the outset

2025: Trimmed back my website to a landing page, ended my sporadic newsletter (which had no real bent anyways), made a blog using Pika to hold reflections
- Writing on Pika was nice because it was *so minimalist*!
- The scope of the blog felt limited - it was JUST a place to write about the creative process, but I don't always have thoughts about the creative process, so I didn't end up turning to it
- I didn't really want to write regular blog posts 
- Every regular blog post required me to write in one editor, copy it into another blog editor, hit post, edit markdown, etc

2026: Well, what is different now?
* Obsidian > Quartz workflow makes me feel like I'm writing in a public Google doc - I just write and write and write and then ta-da, it is shared! (Will I regret this deeply later? Maybe.)
* I don't think of what I am doing in Obsidian as "writing a post." I don't sit down to think "today, I will write a post." I come to Obsidian, I come to this place, because I want to think. I want to play in my own mind. 

## A reason for my failed attempts

was that I was being too narrow and rigid about what *being creative* meant. I was working with tools that required me, or at least encouraged me, to draw lines around my creative practice, and so I drew them. 

My Webflow site with my poetry and fiction wasn't set up to also be a place where I could write about baking or reading, and my attempts to try and make it one were confusing and time-consuming. When I decided to write about "the creative process," I was only describing writing fiction or poetry, not about the other kinds of creativity I practice (exploring things like sewing and baking, but also working as a research professional, which *is*, ideally, creative work). Those other kinds of creativity  became sideshows. My Actual, Serious Creative Work was my [carefully written fiction](https://milkcandyreview.home.blog/2024/11/14/what-i-find-in-my-mothers-house-after-she-dies-by-catherine-swanner/) , and everything else was either my paid job, or a hobby. 

## Small detour: procrastinating on my professional portfolio 
This is probably why, every time I try to update my mostly-dormant UX research portfolio, I stall and procrastinate and give up. The identity and label of "strategic UX researcher with experience working on end-to-end consumer journeys" feels like an itchy sweater. 

## Back to the main idea: isn't it all just work?

What if I decided there isn't a difference between creative writing and hobbies and paid work—[there's only make?](https://www.corita.org/tenrules)  To make sense of the world on one hand, and to make the world I want to live in, on the other? 

*To make sense of the world* brings my research practice into the conversation. (Maybe the paid research I do for my 9-5 less so, but that's a different story...) *To make the world I want to live in* gives me an aspiration to use my creativity to improve the world around me, even in very small ways—like how a set of handsewn napkins brings delight to my boyfriend and I when we sit down for breakfast. 

And then this helps me understand why some projects are bigger than others, not because they are more "legitimate" or "serious" than others (a tendency I am fighting hard right now—the scold's voice in my head says, *do you think people won Pulitzer prizes sewing napkins?*) but because some projects are just trying to take on more. My [[A retro space opera|retro space opera]] is bigger and more time consuming than the napkins because the stakes are not just personal, the pieces of the world I am trying to make sense of are much bigger.
