Earlier I wrote that my creative practice was two fold: [[Everything I make is an expression of my creative practice|to make sense of the world on one hand, and to make the world I want to live in, on the other]]. But in my paid work as a corporate UX researcher, I doubt whether either of those things are happening.

The goal of UX research is always = understand the customer (user) to inform decisions. 

* Decisions = how should we best grow the business? 
* Grow the business = increase profits, scale, etc. 

A good decision should have knowledge behind it, but [[Synthetic personas and other forms of AI participants are like trying to mine for resources in Ringworld|knowledge isn't as interesting as it used to be.]] If we are not in the business of trying to create some sort of real knowledge—[[Historical thinking part 4 - On perspective|partial and fragmented as it may be, as any form of knowledge is]]—then we are not making sense of reality. We are instead passing judgments on vibes.

The other part of my job is providing tools to help people organize information and to take action:
* [[Corporate innovation|jobs-to-be-done]]
* customer journeys

I'm struggling to articulate my thoughts on this right now—these tools are helpful tools to diagnose and remedy problems. They abstract and simplify, like all frameworks do. But I worry that they simplify away the origins, the context, of what is a "customer" or why their "needs" are what they are, treat them as ahistorical and fixed given states. 

If I have the choice to design a research practice of my own:
* that also pays my bills
* (probably by helping other people 1) understand customers or 2) organize information)

then I also want:
* to design a way of working that makes room for, instead of crowds out, these nuances
