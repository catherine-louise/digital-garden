Earlier I wrote that my creative practice was two fold: [[Everything I make is an expression of my creative practice|to make sense of the world on one hand, and to make the world I want to live in, on the other]]. But in my paid work as a corporate UX researcher, I do not think I am doing either of these things, and this is what I want to change.

The goal of UX research is always = inform decisions. 

* Decisions = how should we best grow the business? 
* Grow the business = increase profits, scale, etc. 

A good decision should have knowledge behind it, but [[Synthetic personas and other forms of AI participants are like trying to mine for resources in Ringworld|knowledge isn't as interesting as it used to be.]] If we are not in the business of trying to create some sort of real knowledge—[[Historical thinking part 4 - On perspective|partial and fragmented as it may be, as any form of knowledge is]]—then we are not making sense of reality. We are instead looking at vibes and calling them ["directionally correct"](https://medium.com/weave-lab/five-reasons-why-leaders-never-say-directionally-correct-c1dc719bfb03). 

The other part of my job is providing tools to help people organize information and to take action:
* [[Corporate innovation still perpetuates the world as it is|jobs-to-be-done]]
* customer journeys

I'm struggling to articulate my thoughts on this right now—these tools are helpful tools to diagnose and remedy problems. Where I want to act differently is: I do not want to continue seeing everyone as free agents in a marketplace when I know there are actually many constraints on who can act and when. I do not want to reduce deep cultural scripts and narratives to fungible tokens; instead, I want to think *with* them. I want to speak from a perspective on the world that is clearly mine, instead of reproducing corporate logic in my own practice.
