From [[Space Settlements]] by Fred Scharmen, pp. 116-117:

>A bubble of speculative value in what was once vacuum is made possible by the creation of new empty space where there once was a stock of planetary substance. 
>
>Once this value is extracted and used, it can never be mined again. The explorers of the Ringworld slowly come to the practical realization that this place contains no raw resources in its ground. "Dig on any world," one of the book's explorers points out, "and eventually you will find some kind of metal ore. Here, you will find forty feet of soil, and then the Ring foundation. That material cannot be worked. If it could be pierced, the miner would strike vacuum—a harsh reward for his labor."
>...
>Yet in Niven's novel, civilization has collapsed... the Ringworld's inhabitants are doomed; if they seek new resources in the ground below, they will find only the void.

When I read a "transcript" of an "interview" with a "synthetic participant," and I try to understand why it feels so surreal and unnerving to behold, this passage helps me understand why: because I am perceiving that there is simply no *there,* there. 

The model deploys words that in a traditional interview, I would probe on further. What do you mean by *optimize*? By *best*? By *meets my needs*? But even though I can keep prompting the AI, my prompting is there to generate more text from which I can neatly extract the information the business is looking for. My prompting does not generate or lead to deeper cognitive empathy or understanding of another person's worldview or expertise. I am striking vacuum.

As of this writing I haven't actually interviewed a synthetic participant. I am skeptical of doing so. The exercise both reveals the extractive nature of corporate research (*or perhaps all research...?*) and the profound limitations of an applied research practice where extracting more information quickly is the highest good. The extraction, as in Ringworld, actually already happened, in the form of creating and training the model—but the model has "no raw resources in its ground." All of that was used up to train the model. Extracting information from a "synthetic participant," is actually trying to mine the metal Ring.
