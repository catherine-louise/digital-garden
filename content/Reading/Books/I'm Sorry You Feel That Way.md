Diane Negra
Stanford UP, 2026

The thesis of this book is:

> Relying heavily on sloganization, invoking "convenience" and "progress," the contemporary customer service ethos is emphatically marked by what I characterize as an *antisocial moral economy of self service.*.. customers now face forms of technological conscription with ideological dimensions. (pp 24)
> 
> The poorly served customer, I argue, has come to be the definitional figure of modern commercial relations. 

Then the book investigates:
* what has created this economy
* how it feels to be inside it
* how we represent this culture in media, and therefore, construct and come to understand the larger societal failures behind these customer service modes
* and the consequences:
	* It degrades democracy by habituating customers to seeing themselves *not* as agents but as data points that corporations create, assemble, and use. 
	* It pits people against each other and subjugates them to technological systems that frustrate and disappoint.
