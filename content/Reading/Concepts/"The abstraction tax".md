Coined by Kristen Lowe: https://workbravely.substack.com/p/the-abstraction-tax

Understanding "delegate tasks to AI" through analyzing the history of management (wish I'd thought of that connection myself)

She writes that in an age where we can delegate any tasks to an AI agent, the risk is less that AI makes an error and more that our delegation takes us further and further from the understanding of lived reality that is critical to sound judgment:

> Discernment and decision-making--two things you’ll find on most leadership skill lists--are, after all, mostly a matter of having a really strong grasp on reality and its interrelated pieces and parts. Good judgment is mostly built on seeing the world as it is, free of the filters through which ego, insecurity, and self-interest distort most people’s sense of what is right, and being able to decipher the world in honest, undistorted ways is as much a professional skill as any of the other nebulous things like “taste” that we’re celebrating in the AI era. But, given that we may be headed down a path toward a task-free world where most of what we do is deciding instead of actual doing, it feels particularly critical that we nurse the skill of making sense of what’s real.
